# PAC Monday demo — proposal first

Use the public demo's **Customer proposal** entry point. All sample customer details are fictional. The pricing catalog is PAC-supplied; engineering estimates and approval roles are demonstration assumptions.

## Opening — 45 seconds

“Today we will show the customer conversation first, then the workspace that makes it possible. The goal is a consistent way to understand the application, compare suitable configurations, obtain the right review, and agree what happens next.”

## 1. Present the proposal — 3 minutes

Open the staged deal's proposal. Explain the recorded application and the two alternatives. Compare the standard R3200 configuration with the assisted-loading configuration, including the selected options, source prices and lead-time terms. Do not describe a reference photograph or video as proof of the exact quoted configuration.

“These are two alternatives within one opportunity. Each can have its own revisions. The customer can compare them without receiving a disconnected collection of PDFs.”

Record a customer question against an issued quote revision. Select a preferred alternative. Explain that preference is a discussion outcome, not a signed order.

## 2. Open the sales workspace — 2 minutes

Show the same deal, its owner, status, recorded scope, quotes and next action. Open the issued quote that the customer just reviewed.

“The application, proposal feedback and sales follow-up belong to the same deal. A rep can see what was presented and what still needs attention.”

## 3. Revise and configure — 3 minutes

Create a new revision with a reason. Add a standard option and show any required companion option and the combined price before confirming. Show the price-visibility control for a customer conversation.

Add an **Engineering requirement** describing what is custom and why. Leave it unpriced first and show the issue blocker. Switch the clearly labeled demo role to Engineering, enter labor hours and material cost, and explain the single illustrative labor rate. Review the application and engineering pricing. If a discount is introduced, demonstrate the additional manager review.

“A missing engineering price is visible. It cannot silently become zero or be issued as a final quote. An approval belongs to the version that was actually reviewed.”

Change a priced field after review to show the approvals clear. Leave the revised quote open while you capture the supporting 3D evidence. Final review and issue follow that attachment.

## 4. Discover a requirement in 3D — 2 minutes

From the same deal, open **3D Explorer**. Select a component, adjust the view, and capture the selection. Review the evidence and explicitly attach it to the deal or quote. Return to the deal, reopen the saved evidence, and restore the view.

“This reference assembly demonstrates the evidence workflow. It is not a PAC machine model. With approved PAC CAD, the same mechanism could preserve the machine or option being discussed.”

## 5. Close the loop — 1 minute

Return from Explorer to the working quote. Complete the required reviews against its final scope and attached evidence, issue the new revision, and compare it with the preserved original. Return to the proposal and show the newly issued revision, its matching total, attached evidence, and revision-specific comments. Open the print/PDF view. Reload the workspace and verify the deal, issued versions, feedback and evidence remain available.

Set a named owner, next action and due date.

“The output is a proposal we can discuss, plus a sales record that tells us what to do next.”

## Decisions to capture with PAC

| Decision | Proposed owner | Required confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pilot application and alternatives | Sales + engineering | Machine families, product samples, bag format and required information |
| Engineering pricing | Engineering + commercial owner | Labor rate, whether it represents cost or selling price, material treatment and final pricing authority |
| Review rules | Sales leadership | Who reviews applications, engineering exceptions, discounts and price overrides; who may bypass which gates |
| CRM workflow | CRM administrator | CRM name, required deal fields, quote-number convention and integration boundary |
| Media | Product/engineering owner | Approved model-specific photos, videos and drawings; availability of PAC CAD |
| Pilot acceptance | Project owner | Named test cases, source-price reconciliation, approvals/revisions, proposal feedback and follow-up expectations |

Record each decision with an owner and due date. Unresolved decisions remain open; do not convert internal timing or commercial ideas into PAC commitments.

## Demonstration boundaries

This build is browser-local. Role switching, deal references and customer comments demonstrate the workflow on one browser. There is no connected CRM, SSO, email approval delivery, multi-user synchronization, e-signature or payment processing. Public GitHub Pages hosting does not make locally saved deals shareable across devices. Keep a JSON backup before clearing browser data.

## Rehearsal acceptance

- Two alternatives are distinct from their revision histories.
- Missing scope, unresolved prices and missing reviews prevent issue.
- Commercial edits invalidate approval of the changed draft.
- Issued revisions remain unchanged when a new revision is edited.
- Proposal and PDF use issued snapshots and matching totals.
- Comments identify the quote and revision they discuss.
- Explorer evidence returns to the same deal and restores its saved view.
- Reload retains deals, revisions, comments and evidence.
- Desktop and phone views remain readable and keyboard actions retain focus.
